# Architecture

Vanilla JavaScript ES modules served by Vite 8. No framework. Strudel runs inside the page through the `<strudel-editor>` web component from `@strudel/repl` 1.3.0.

## Data flow

1. The library merges built-in tracks with the owner's saved tracks (`localStorage` key `coding-misk-library`). Saved copies of built-ins override them by id.
2. Built-in songs are the JSON files in `songs/` (loaded with `import.meta.glob`, validated, ordered by `songs/index.json`). Saved songs from older versions (scenes) are converted with `fromScenes` by `prepare()`.
3. The song being edited (`T`, format v2), the selected section `sel` and the selected track `tk` live in `coding-misk-draft`. The arranger shows one row per track, in two views of the same data: sections (a cell is a clip covering that section) and free timeline (clips positioned by bar, created, moved and resized with the pointer or the keyboard; `selClip` is the selected clip). The track panel is built per track (`panelHtml(i)` inside a `[data-ptrk]` host, element ids prefixed `p<i>_`), so the full view can show many at once; any action inside a panel first selects that track. It edits the selected track: its settings (for the whole track, or only the selected section through the clip's `set`), its patterns and which pattern plays in the selected section.
4. Any change calls `changed()`: mark dirty, recompile with `compileSong`, `ed.setCode`, re-evaluate if playing (without stopping). Muted tracks, and non-solo tracks when any track is soloed, are left out of the code.
5. `playSong(sg, bar, mode)` stops, sets the editor code (with `setcpm` replaced by the start bar tempo), sets `scheduler.lastEnd = bar` to start from any bar, then evaluates.
6. The `transport()` loop runs every animation frame: applies the per-bar tempo with `scheduler.setCps`, handles section loop and end of track, follows the playing section in the arranger (not while a field of the song is being edited), updates buttons, timelines and the master gain (0.6).

Modes: `track` (song from the arranger) and `free` (lessons, sounds, hand-written tracks).

## Strudel integration notes

- Audio worklets load on the first real mouse down. `initAudioOnce()` calls `initAudio()` inside the Play gesture so keyboard and scripted starts work.
- The REPL preloads only part of dirt-samples; the app calls `samples('github:tidalcycles/dirt-samples')` and custom samples from `/samples/strudel.json`.
- Globals used after load: `getAudioContext`, `getSuperdoughAudioController`, `getAnalyzerData`, `analysers`, `samples`, `initAudio`, `soundMap`.
- Master output node: `getSuperdoughAudioController().output.destinationGain`. Used for WAV export (MediaRecorder on a MediaStreamDestination) and demo recording. The master level and the master limiter sit just before it (level-headroom, `src/sounds/limiter.js`): `channelMerger → level (MASTER × the style's push) → limiter worklet → guard → destinationGain (gain 1)`, installed by `applyMaster` every frame (again when superdough makes a new output) and by the render page, so everything that taps `destinationGain` gets the limited sound.

## Visuals

`startVisuals` reads every analyser in `window.analysers`, sums them into the waveform, and keeps a normalised level and onset detector per instrument id: `kick`, `snare`, `hats`, `fx`, `bass`, `guitar`, `arp`, `pad`, `hook`, `riser` (plus `1` for untagged code). Each scene function in `SCENE_DRAW` draws one theme; see `docs/DESIGN-SYSTEM.md`.

The Soul look (`#46`) is drawn by `src/soul/`: `data.js` (pure, checked in Node: `soulData(song, ctx)` builds the soul from a radio song with its plan, or measures any v2 song part by part with the radio's measures; seeded `hashOf` and timed `eventAt`; default view from the seed), `views.js` (`createSoulRenderer`, the ten views and the overlays on an offscreen canvas), `scene.js` (`createSoulScene`: follows the song playing through `getSource`, analyzes a new song, recalibrates when steering replaces the song object, view, lock, fit into the stage). The radio gives `soulSource(cyc)`; elsewhere the playing song's `doc` (or the first song of a mix) is used.

The Soul display (`#46` phase 2) lives in a bay beside the stage, inside `#stagerow`: `src/soul/display.js` (`createSoulDisplay`: the hidden screw and the typed "soul", the open and close timeline, the display's controls, the shell selector, the screen with its power effects; it draws the soul through the scene's `frame()`, so stage and display share one render), `src/soul/shells.js` (the seven shells as SVG from the v4 mockups, shared gradients and filters in one hidden SVG with `sd-` ids) and `src/soul/cable.js` (the SCART cable: a verlet rope on a fixed canvas over the page, pinned to the sockets under the bay and the stage, draggable). With override on, `main.js` makes the stage draw the soul without changing the chosen visual.

## Persistence keys

`coding-misk-library`, `coding-misk-draft`, `coding-misk-shape-cap` (growth shapes tried in Compose, shape-search; absent means 8), `coding-misk-look`, `coding-misk-ui`, `coding-misk-lang`, `coding-misk-tab`, `coding-misk-code-w` (code panel width), `coding-misk-code-collapsed`, `coding-misk-panel` (track panel view: `one`, `all` summary or `full`), `coding-misk-arr-mode` (arranger view: `sections` or `timeline`), `coding-misk-snd-view` (sound browser view), `coding-misk-favs` (favourite sounds), `coding-misk-live` (live build switch for songs without steps). `coding-misk-patterns` (the user's saved patterns, see Patterns tab).

Growths made in Compose are kept in IndexedDB, not in localStorage: the database `coding-misk-growths` (`src/live/growth-store.js`, growth-in-background), at most 60 grown songs, the least recently used dropped first, keyed by the Compose growth key, the voice instrument's context and the growth hash of `src/song/`, `src/live/`, `src/endless/` and `styles/` (`virtual:growth-hash` from `vite.config.js`). A new hash empties it. It is a cache: not in the backup, and a blocked store only means the song grows again.

`coding-misk-library` holds `{ tracks, code, codeSongs, trash, hidden }`. `tracks`: the user's composed songs and their edits of built-in songs; `code`: edits of hand-written songs and versions; `codeSongs`: hand-coded versions (`#33`); `trash` (`#68`): deleted songs as `[{ id, kind, song, deletedAt }]`, `kind` `track` or `version`, `song` the stored object as it was (a version with its edited code), `deletedAt` an ISO date. A library without `trash` reads as an empty trash. The logic is pure in `src/library/trash.js` (`trashSong`, `restoreSong`, `deleteForGood`, `emptyTrash`, `purgeTrash`, `daysLeft`, `mergeTrash`; checked by `npm run check:trash`): entries 30 days old are purged at start and when the Trash view of the Songs tab opens. A delete also calls `removeSong(id)` of the playlist store, so no playlist keeps the id; a restore does not add it back. The backup merges `trash` by id and drops any entry whose id is alive in `tracks` or `codeSongs` on either side, so an import never deletes a song. Built-in songs are hidden, not deleted (D3b of `song-trash`): `hidden` lists their ids, `composedTracks()` and `codedTracks()` leave them out (so the Songs list, the Compose song menu and the playlists' song list skip them), and the trash holds `{ id, kind: 'builtin', title, deletedAt }` with no copy. `restoreSong` removes the id from `hidden`; delete for good and the purge keep it; `showHidden` (Settings → Library) clears `hidden` and the `builtin` entries. The backup merges `hidden` as a union and keeps `builtin` entries even when an edit of the song is in `tracks`.

`tools/record-demo.cjs` warms up samples, then records a scripted tour with a fake cursor overlay, capturing audio from the master node. Merge:

```sh
OFF=$(cat out/offset.txt)
ffmpeg -ss $OFF -i out/video.webm -i out/audio.webm -map 0:v -map 1:a -c:v libx264 -crf 20 -pix_fmt yuv420p \
  -af "alimiter=limit=0.89:level=false" -c:a aac -b:a 192k -shortest -movflags +faststart demo.mp4
```

Lessons from recording: clicking a native `<select>` opens a popup that swallows the next click (set the value programmatically and draw the click ripple yourself); controls below the 900 px fold cannot be clicked by coordinates; start playback with the top Play button.

## Live build

A song with `build` steps (`src/song/build.js`) is played by state: `stateAt(song, bar)` applies the steps up to that bar, and the playable object gets `codeAt(bar)` (compiled state with the latest comment as `// > …`). `playSong` loads the code of the starting bar. During playback `transport()` calls `liveBuild()`: during the bar before the next step it animates the editor from the current code to the next one (`src/song/typing.js`: unchanged lines stay, changed lines are edited in place, new lines typed), keeps the edit in view, then evaluates just before the step's bar so the change sounds on the beat. The comment of the latest step shows over the stage (`#say`). Track buses are assigned before muted tracks are skipped, so a track coming in later does not move the others.

The "Live build" switch (`coding-misk-live`) gives songs without steps a derived build (`deriveBuild`): add and remove only at clip edges, removals delayed past a section's fade, so the audio does not change. `buildMap` gives the timeline the silent spans and the step marks of each track.

Crescendo (`src/song/crescendo.js`, #64): `crescendoSong(song, { seed, narrated })` returns an ordinary song with a growth span in front, generated pattern variants and a `build`, so nothing downstream changes. Per track, `trackPlan` gives a minimal state (setup steps at bar 0, while the track is silent) and two queues of small units (pattern fullness, then colour: knobs toward their written value and rack devices); `crescendoPath` interleaves them from the anchor track on, the same order as a teardown played backwards. The growth repeats the reference section (the first where most tracks play) within 1.5 to 2 times the written length; tracks that enter later grow after their first clip. `playable()` uses it when the song's build style (`buildStyle`, the per-song override, or `coding-misk-live-style`) is crescendo, cached per song version; `coding-misk-live-narrated` makes every step speak. The arranger shows the written song: `growBars()` and `arrSec()` map its bars and sections to the played song. `settleOthers` (`typing.js`) makes the edits outside the playing section at once, so only the playing line is typed, also in the video feed. Fragments of a Crescendo song start on a step (`shape.steps`). Compact code (`src/song/compile-compact.js`, `coding-misk-live-code`): `compileCompact(state, { muted })` parses the standard output and joins, per track, the layers with the same code whose lanes never overlap, under a new lane named after the track; tracks the build has yet to bring in are compiled too and written with `_$:`. `check:crescendo` compares layers and lane sums of both outputs. With `lines: 'track'` it also joins layers with the same skeleton (literals replaced; bus, visual, bank, masks and time functions kept) whose lanes never overlap: numbers become `"<a!n b!m>"` sequences, strings `"<0!n 1!m>".pick([…])`. `tools/check-compact-code.cjs` compares the Strudel events of the views in the browser. The code of the moment (`compileNow(state, bar, { prepare, shownMuted })`, code-now) keeps the layers that sound in the section holding the bar, drops lanes that are 1 there, and with `prepare` adds the next section's new layers as `_$:`; `playable()` adds a step at each section boundary and one or two bars before it, and takes the tempo map from the code per section. The idiomatic generator (`src/song/idiom.js`, `coding-misk-live-gen`) is a text pass on the final code: drum rows of drum machines to their coarsest grid and smallest repeating cell, note groups to repeated cells and `!`, and a line that is the same on every chord to `"steps".add("<roots>")`. `CRESCENDO_VERSION` goes up when the fixtures of `npm run check:crescendo` change; `tools/check-crescendo-play.cjs` checks it in the browser.

Typing (live-typing): `typingPlan(from, to, { speed, max })` in `typing.js` diffs lines, then tokens inside a changed line, and schedules deletes and typing per changed place (8 to 30 characters a second by edit length, deletes three times faster, short pauses), scaled down to the time available. `liveBuild` builds the plan right after the previous step (`makeTyping`) and starts it as late as it can before the step; resuming after hand takeover and the fast video's feed (`codeFrom(segments, { seconds, speed })`) use the same plan. `coding-misk-typing` sets slow, normal or fast. The code panel glides to the edit (`glideTo`).

Natural growth (natural-growth): `crescendoSong(song, { rules: { natural: true } })`, used by the Live coder profile (id `switch`), hides at bar 0 the functions a track writes (`hide` steps; `track.hidden` makes `compileSong` leave them out with `stripFns`) and reveals them one per step before their knob moves; some knob moves are tried past their value first; steps are 4 to 8 bars apart then 2 to 6, with a pause of 4 to 8 bars after entries, the progression and the drums' return, kept when the growth is squeezed.

Performance profiles (`src/live/profiles.js`): `PROFILES` (classic, crescendo, switch) hold the controls (build, growth, narrated, typing, view, gen, title, comments, highlights); `resolveProfile(id, overrides)` gives the controls in force and whether the profile is custom. In `main.js` `profileFor(tr)` takes the session override of the song, then the song's `profile` (or its old `buildStyle`), then `coding-misk-profile`; changes by hand go to `coding-misk-profile-overrides` (`setControl`), picking a profile clears them. `applyComments` (`src/song/comments.js`) applies the title and the comment level to the code of every bar; `compileNow` keeps the header of a track that came in (`keepHeaders`). The video code panel lights the playing layers from `visuals.levels` when the profile asks (`getHighlights`).

Pinned settings: `stateAt` restores a track's own value for every key in `track.pinned` after applying the steps. In the panel, a pin next to a knob shows when steps change that value; changing it pins it (`setSetting`), clicking the pin releases it. Voice pitch and tempo are independent: `speed` sets the length and `stretch` (Strudel's phase vocoder) corrects the pitch.

Spoken comments: `tools/voice.mjs` writes `public/samples/say_<lang>/<slug>.wav` with macOS `say` (git-ignored). The custom samples manifest maps them to `s("say_en").n(k)`; `voiceCode` adds a one-shot layer masked to the step's bar with the settings and rack of the step's voice track (taken from the state at that bar; `compileSong` skips voice tracks), and `playSong` loads every voice sample silently first. A step is evaluated when the scheduler's `lastEnd` is about to reach its bar, so the first hit of the bar already comes from the new code.

Voice instrument (voice-instrument, #39): `src/song/spoken.js` places the lines of `spoken` tracks (`placeSpoken`, from the clips only, so every state of a live build agrees) and `compileSong` writes them as sample layers on orbits 13 and 14. What the compiler knows about the machine lives in `spokenContext`: the samples manifest (`files`; `null` in Node, where every sample is assumed and numbered by the song's sorted texts, so snapshots do not depend on the machine), the sample lengths of `public/samples/say_lengths.json`, the language and the style recipes. `main.js` sets it when the manifest loads and recompiles the song in Compose. A filtered song (the fast export keeps only the tracks of a block) carries `whole`, the full song, so quiet sections stay the same. `voiceCode` skips a narration on a bar where a spoken line sounds, and with `narrator: "poetic"` speaks a short image (`src/song/vocabulary.js`) before each entry instead of the step's comment.

## Endless director

`generateSession(recipes, options)` returns `{ session, songs }`: plain v2 songs with `build` steps, so the player, the live build engine, the voice track and the exporter play them unchanged. For each song the director plans parts, tempo, key, sections and candidate tracks, then walks phrase boundaries: it applies candidate moves to a copy of the steps, measures the result with `stateAt` and `energyOf`, and keeps the move closest to the target energy. All randomness comes from named seeded streams, never `Math.random`. `tools/endless.mjs` writes songs to `songs/endless/` (git-ignored); the library picks them up through the `songs/**` glob, which skips `session.json`. The radio view (`#22`) will call the same functions in the browser.

Radio recording (`#30`): `startCapture()` in `src/main.js` taps the master output (before the volume) into a `MediaRecorder` (Opus or, decoded after the recording, WAV, from the settings), with pause and resume. ## Player bar and volume

The bottom bar (`#pbar`) holds `#play` and `#stop` (moved from the top bar with their handlers), previous and next, title and position, ON AIR, volume and mute. `renderPlayerBar()` runs in `transport()` and redraws only when its text changes. Previous and next walk the Songs tab order (`libraryCards()`) and play the neighbour if music was playing; in the radio they call `radio.restart()` and `radio.skip()`.

The volume is a `GainNode` inserted once between superdough's `destinationGain` and the audio destination (`applyVolume`, checked every frame like `MASTER`). The WAV recorder taps `destinationGain`, before the volume, so exports keep their level. Keys: `coding-misk-volume` (0 to 100), `coding-misk-muted`.

## Hand live coding

A keystroke that edits (or a paste, cut or drop) in `#edhost` during a live build sets `hand` (`takeOver`). By hand `transport()` skips `liveBuild()`, the comment overlay, the stop at the end and `radio.tick()`; the tempo map goes on. `changed()` recompiles the song without touching the editor. Resume (`resumeHand`) keeps the editor code in `lastHand` (session storage, "Your last code" block). With "from where I was" (`coding-misk-hand-from`, default on) `resumeBuild()` types back to the song's code at `handFrom`, the bar of the takeover, and on the next bar plays the song from there (`playSong`); otherwise it types back to `codeFor(song, bar)` and evaluates on the next bar, and past the end of a song in Compose plays from the top; in the radio `radio.afterHand(bar)` starts the next song when the song on air ended. Pause and resume keep the hand code (`playSong(..., { keepHand: true })`); any other playback, skip or stop leaves the hand mode.

## Radio

The radio is a third player mode, `radio`, next to `track` and `free`. It plays a window song (`windowSong`: the song on air and the next one, on absolute stream bars), so the scheduler never restarts. `transport()` calls `radio.tick(cyc)` every frame and, in radio mode, does not stop at the end of the window. The radio:

- generates the next song in an idle callback once the song on air has played 40 % of it, then hands the player a new window (`swap`: steps already played are not repeated);
- moves the window when a song ends, and adds the new song to the history;
- on skip, cuts the song on air at the next bar and evaluates the new window at once (`jump`).

Starting any other playback, the header pause or stop, or "open in Compose" stops the radio: they share one editor and scheduler. Recipes reach the browser through `import.meta.glob('../styles/*.json')`; invalid ones are left out. Browser storage keys: `coding-misk-radio` (controls), `coding-misk-radio-history` (last 50 songs with their JSON and session recipe).

## Fast export (`#8`)

`src/export/fast.js` opens a fresh hidden iframe on `render.html` (`src/render/page.js`) for each export. The page loads its own Strudel (the editor's prebake, Dirt-Samples, `/samples/strudel.json`) after setting an `OfflineAudioContext`, so every global effect belongs to it; superdough keeps one context and pools filter and compressor nodes, which is why the page is never reused. The job is `{ segments, bpm, tail, sampleRate, gain, push }`: code segments from `segmentsOf` (the whole song, or one per live build step), the tempo map per bar (`src/render/tempo.js`), the master level and the style's push into the master limiter (the page installs the same limiter as the app and renders its 5 ms look-ahead longer, then drops it, so the file is not delayed). The page evaluates each segment with a Strudel `repl` that never starts, queries its haps, and calls `superdough` two seconds ahead of the render through `suspend()` (all at once makes the offline context process thousands of idle nodes). It posts progress, then the channels (transferred) and the onsets per instrument. `src/export/encode.js` writes WAV and loads Mediabunny with its LAME and libFLAC encoders (WebCodecs for Opus in Ogg) for the other formats. Settings keys: `coding-misk-export-format` (`wav`, `flac`, `mp3`, `opus`), `coding-misk-export-mp3` (320, 192), `coding-misk-export-speed` (`fast`, `real`). A failed render falls back to the real time export. Exports render in blocks (`renderInBlocks`: 4 minutes each, 8 seconds of lead in, 20 ms crossfade) into a streaming encoder (`createEncoder`), one fresh render page per block. The radio's export (`radio.exportItems(range)`: up to now, next N songs, minutes) joins the songs like a saved session (`joinSession`).

Timeline JSON (format 1), saved as `<title>.timeline.json`: `{ format, app, title, seconds, bars, bpm[], sections[{ label, bar, bars, t }], code[{ bar, t, code }], say[{ bar, t, text }], instruments{ name: [[t, length, level]] } }`, times in seconds from the start of the audio file. It is the input of the fast video (`#53`).

Long exports (`#61`): above `LONG_SECONDS` (10 minutes) `askDiskFile` (`src/export/disk.js`) opens the browser's save dialog right after the click and returns a `FileSystemWritableFileStream`. `createEncoder(..., { writable })` writes WAV parts at their offset (header last, at 0) or gives Mediabunny a `StreamTarget`; `renderVideo({ writable })` does the same for MP4 (moov at the end), so memory stays flat. Without the API the file is kept in memory as before.

Sound guard (`#54`, `src/sounds/sound-guard.js`): `guardDecode` wraps `decodeAudioData` so a failure without an error callback (Strudel's soundfont loader) reaches the success callback as `null` and the note fails instead of waiting forever. `guardSounds` wraps every soundfont and sample sound of the sound map (and those registered later, through `soundMap.listen`): the register (`createRegister`: `SOUNDFONT_FIX` plus learned entries in `localStorage` under `coding-misk-sound-health`, keyed by the soundfonts package version) maps a broken font, zone or sample to a stand-in; a note that fails with a decode or parse error is learned (a soundfont's broken zone range is read from its font file) and played again from the stand-in. Network errors are not learned. Sample banks get their addresses fixed in place before the first note (`fixSampleBank`: VCSL from jsDelivr, the `tom_mallet` path, `#` and a lone `%` encoded). The app and the render page install it; the page's pre-flight plays every distinct sound of a block once past its end before rendering, and reports `skipped` and `replaced`. The radio's `swap` calls `preloadFonts` for the next song's soundfonts (one silent note per zone, with pauses, in the app page: a hidden render page held the main thread for 1.7 s). `tools/check-sounds.cjs` checks every zone and sample of the library; `npm run check:sound-guard` checks the pure parts.

## Fast video (`#53`)

`src/export/fast-video.js` (`renderVideo`) runs `renderInBlocks` with a sink that, for each audio block, adds the audio to a Mediabunny `AudioSampleSource` and draws the block's frames: `drawFrame(t, cyc, feed, codeAt)` in `main.js` sets `fv` (the frame's info, code, voice line and soul source, read by `stageInfo`, the soul scene and the video composer instead of the live player), calls `visuals.drawAt({ now, cyc, look, feed })` and `video.composeAt()`, then the frame goes to a `CanvasSource`. The feed (`src/video/feed.js`) has per instrument levels from the onsets (`levelsFrom`), the waveform from the rendered audio, the code typed during the bar before each step (`codeFrom`, the same `typingFrames` as live) and the voice lines (`sayFrom`). `visuals.hold(true)` pauses the live loop; `soul.setClock()` gives the soul the frame's time. The direction lives in `coding-misk-video` (`visual`, `soul`, with `lookFor`) and `coding-misk-video-presets`. MP4 (H.264 + AAC) when WebCodecs can, else WebM (VP9 + Opus).

## Fragments and texts for sharing (`#57`)

`src/video/fragments.js` (pure) scores each bar by the tracks playing in it and its section's kind (`barScores`), and `pickFragments` draws seeded, non-overlapping windows on bar lines from the best ones; `fragmentBetween` checks a from–to; `fragmentCode` and `fragmentLabel` make the CRT label; `glitchMessages` and `glitchAt` the glitch frames. `verticalAreas` in `src/video/layout.js` lays out the 1080×1920 picture (`v-code`, `v-soul`, `v-bands`, `v-soul-bands`). `makeFragments` in `main.js` resolves the source (`fragmentSource`: a song id, a playlist session id, or a radio recipe object with its `export: { from, count, cut }`), picks the stretches and calls `exportVideoFast(sg, title, { frag })` once per fragment: the job gets `start`, `cut`, `fadeIn` and `fade`, `renderInBlocks` starts its first block `LEAD` seconds before `start` and counts output times (onsets included) from it, and `renderVideo` draws frame `t` at the song's cycle `start + t` with `video.composeVertical` (bands, logo with the equaliser, label, glitch). `src/video/texts.js` (pure) writes a title and a description from the source's facts (`textsInfo`: song titles and starts, genres, length, seed); `saveTexts` saves them as `.txt` next to every fragment and every fast export, `showTexts` opens the panel to copy and draw again. Console: `globalThis.codingMiskClips` (`fragments`, `texts`, `sources`, `job`, `next`). Keys: `coding-misk-fragments` (vertical settings), `coding-misk-fragment-n` (next label number).

Taglines and upload fields (`fragment-extras`): `TAGLINES` and `taglineFor(code)` give a random-mode fragment its own phrase; `taglineAt(text, t, code)` is the typed tagline frame (characters, block cursor, short glitches), drawn by `drawBottomBand`. `textsFor` adds the tagline, the song's `inspiration` (song JSON, `{ en, it }`), `tags`, `hashtags` and `category`; `textFile` writes them under the description.

## Live output (`#52`)

Timeline views (#65): `src/live/timeline.js` draws the timeline (`drawTimeline`: rows, clips, sections, playhead, step marks and the latest comment, whole song or a scrolling window) and the track column (`drawColumn`) on a canvas from a song shape and a bar. `liveAreas` gives every view a `timeline` and a `column` area (hidden off the picture when unused); presets 6 to 11 use them, and the same views are video layouts (`TIMELINE_LAYOUTS`, drawn through `paintLive`). A preset's `future` (`auto`, `show`, `hide`) decides whether what is coming shows; `auto` hides it for songs with a live build. `timelineNow` in `main.js` takes the radio's song on air (its own bars) or the song playing, shapes cached per song object, so a steering rewrite redraws.

Three pages of the same origin. The app tab composes the live picture: `video.live` in `src/video/video.js` draws the stage, code, soul panel and song card into any canvas, with the areas of the preset on air (`src/live/presets.js`: five views, each with or without the card) and a 0.8 s transition (`lerpAreas`). `src/live/link.js` sets `globalThis.codingMiskLive`. ## Patterns tab

`pattern-library` (`#72`). `src/library/patterns.js` is pure (checked in Node by `npm run check:patterns`); `src/library/patterns-tab.js` draws the tab in the Groove Lab, after Sounds (tab id `pattern`).

- **Catalogue.** `builtinPatterns()` reads every preset once, by kind: melodies (`HOOKS`, track type hook), basslines (`BASS` beat templates), arpeggios (`ARPS`), chords (`PADS` rhythms, played over the progression chosen in the detail), grooves (`GROOVES`, kept as their idea), riffs (`BASS` riffs on bass and `GUITAR_PATTERNS` on guitar). Each item has a key `<kind>/<name>` and its defaults: a minor progression, a sound of its kind, 120 to 140 BPM, the beat on for basslines and riffs. `stylesUsing(recipes)` maps each key to the styles whose recipes name the preset (`drums.grooves`, `*.presets`, `guitar.patterns`).
- **Loop.** `loopSong(item, cfg)` builds a song in memory: one section of 4 bars with the chosen key, progression, meter and tempo, the pattern's track with the chosen sound (and the track's own settings when it comes from Compose), and, with the beat, a drums track with a kick on every beat and an offbeat hat at gain 0.4. `main.js` compiles it with `compileSong`, writes it with the Technical functions preset of the current profile (`writeCode`, the hidden mixer tail when needed) and plays it with the song player (`playLoop`); the song has `loop: true`, so `songTick` never ends it, and a change while it plays is evaluated when the scheduler reaches the next bar. Playing a pattern stops a song or the radio first; the code panel says `Pattern: <name>`.
- **Preview.** `previewOf(type, pattern, ctx)` reads the idea (degrees, chord indices, rhythm cells, semitones on top, seeded random indices, drum rows, with the mini-notation reader of `src/song/ideas.js`) over one to four bars: notes as MIDI numbers against time, or a grid of drum rows. The tab draws it on a canvas with the theme tokens and lights the step the player is on.
- **Store.** Browser key `coding-misk-patterns`, a list of `{ id, name, kind, type, pattern, sound, settings, context { key, chords, meter, bpm, mode }, styles, source ('compose' or 'generated'), seed, why, created }`. `patternStore(store)` adds, renames and removes; built-in patterns are never in it. The backup merges the key by id (`mergeBackup`, count `patterns`). Save as pattern in Compose (`savedFromTrack`) takes the context of the first section where one of the pattern's clips plays and the song's style tags. "New" in the detail calls `compose(kind, { seed: "pattern:<kind>:<number>", style, harmony, energy })`; the part waits in a "new" strip, unsaved, until Save, and the strip empties when the tab closes.
- **Links.** Compose: a listen button next to a track's preset menu (or groove menu) opens the pattern with the track's sound and settings, the section's key and progression and its tempo, and stops the song; a pattern with no preset opens as an unsaved item. Sounds: [Try on a pattern] on an instrument or synth (bass sounds on a bassline, keys and pads on chords, the rest on a melody). Styles: preset names in a sheet open the pattern; the detail's styles open the Styles tab. Add to song (`addToSong`) puts the pattern on the next free letter of the first track of its type, or on a new track (default settings, the pattern's sound, a clip in the selected section); Compose marks the song changed.
- **Radio.** `forRadio(list)` gives the director the user's patterns (type, pattern, style tags or `any`, context, idea); `createRadio({ userPatterns })` adds them to the options of each song, so they are saved with the session recipe and a replay draws the same songs (`src/endless/material.js`, `userPool`).
- **Follow-ups** (`pattern-followups`). A saved pattern's styles are chips with an add menu and a remove cross (`patternStore().setStyles`, `cleanStyles`: `any` only alone, `[]` means every style); Save as pattern falls back to `fallbackStyles(song, { recipes, artists })` when the song has no style tags. New's parts are drafts in `src/library/pattern-drafts.js` (browser key `coding-misk-pattern-drafts`, at most 30, newest first, merged by id in the backup), with Discard and Discard all through `confirmInline`. `composeNewPart()` writes New's part; on riffs a guitar riff (`compose('chords', { type: 'guitar', riff: true })`) or a bass riff. Compose's guitar tracks choose Riff or Chords (`riff: true`/`false`) next to Compose a part.
- **Favourites and votes, later** (design D8). Their place: a star on each card, and in the detail a vote of three values (no, maybe, yes) stored per pattern next to the rules that made it (`why` and `seed` for generated patterns, the preset name for built-in ones). The material generator will read the votes as weights per rule and per kind. Saved generated patterns already keep `why` and `seed`, so they can be voted later.

## Sound browser

`buildCatalog(soundMap, customBanks)` reads every loaded sound. Keys in `soundMap` are lowercase and drum machines also have aliases (`tr909_bd` next to `rolandtr909_bd`): only the 71 canonical machines of `machines.js` are kept. Sample banks keep loading after start-up (the full dirt-samples archive arrives a few seconds later), so the browser re-reads the catalogue every second until it stops growing. Pads play one shot through `superdough()`; cards and lists play a looping audition through the editor. "Use" sets the kit of a drums track, the sound of a bass, arp, hook or pad track, or the sample of a texture track (the selected track first, otherwise the first suitable one).

"Machine groove" (`grooveCode`) plays every sound of a drum machine in 4 bars: bars 1-2 the basic beat (kick, snare or clap, hats), bars 3-4 every other sound in its usual role (crash, ride, clap, tambourine, rimshot, cowbell, percussion, shaker, misc, effects), and a tom fill from high to low at the end of bar 4. Each line of the code carries a comment with its role. Variants are left out; the browser loads every sound of the machine before it starts, so the one-shot crash and fill are not skipped as late. While anything plays, the chip, card, row or pad of each sound lights up on every hit: each animation frame queries the scheduler's pattern for the hits since the previous frame.
