# Visuals

## What it is

What the app draws while music plays, and how video files are laid out.

- **Themes.** Two interface themes: `neon` (default) and `hw`, a hardware look with knobs and LEDs (`data-ui="hw"`, stored in `coding-misk-ui`). Tokens and components are in `docs/DESIGN-SYSTEM.md`.
- **Looks.** Canvas visuals on the stage, one per look, drawn by `src/visuals.js`. Each look reacts to the level and the onsets of every instrument (`.analyze()` names).
- **Widget looks** (`#79`, OpenSpec change `widgets-on-stage`). Monitor (a panel per playing instrument, up to 6, each with its code widget, name, level bar and onset flash, dimmed while silent) and Rullo (en: Roll; the melodic instruments on one pitch axis around a playhead, a legend, kick, snare and hats as lanes of dots). Drawn by `src/visuals/widget-looks.js` with the shared painters of `src/visuals/widget-painters.js`, from the stage feed of `src/visuals/stage-feed.js` and a palette of `src/visuals/widget-palettes.js` (neon or hardware, one colour per instrument). Instruments and widgets come from the Code widgets card, whether or not it is on; the panels change only at a section start, with a short fade. Monitor's grid: `monitorLayout` (1 to the Monitor limit of the Stage widgets card, 6 by default; 3 or fewer bigger and centred; 3×2 on a wide stage, 2×3 on a tall one). The Stage widgets card (Settings, `src/visuals/stage-settings.js`) can give the stage its own instruments section, colours and silent instruments.
- **Layer over the other looks** (widgets-on-stage D4, `src/visuals/widget-layer.js`). Off by default: then nothing is asked or drawn and every look is the same picture as before (hashes of the deterministic looks equal to develop's, `tools/check-stage-widgets.cjs --hashes=`). Strip: up to 4 see-through panels (Monitor's `drawPanel`, panel at 60% opacity) drawn after the look in `paint` and before the flash, so every video and the live output have them: on a wide stage two per side stacked around the middle, about a fifth of the width and height each (`stripLayout`: left top, right top, left bottom, right bottom; 3 panels are two left and one right at the same top, 1 on the left), on a tall stage a centred row at the bottom (up to 3, 4 as 2×2). Palco: on the Palco look the panels fill its LED wall (`screenLayout` inside `palcoScreen`, the wall's proportions, up to 4, fewer bigger), the strip on the others. Never on Monitor, Rullo and the soul. Colours: the playing look's `PAL` through `scenePalette` (automatic), or the app theme. Cost: about 0.2 ms a frame at 1080p for 4 panels (budget 1.5 ms, 2 ms for the screen).
- **Render styles** (`#79`, OpenSpec change `widget-render-styles`). A second choice beside the look, "Render" in both languages (the word "style" names the music recipes): Normal (default, nothing drawn), 1-bit, CRT, LED, ASCII, Terminal (`RENDERS` in `src/music.js`, saved in `coding-misk-render`). Any look takes any render. `paint` in `src/visuals.js` draws the look and its flash, then a pass over the finished frame on the canvas drawn (`createRenderer` in `src/visuals/render-styles.js`): 1-bit reads the frame at a third of its size, lifts it with a black point and a gain per look (`RENDER_GAIN`) and dithers it with an 8×8 Bayer matrix in two theme colours (neon paper white, hardware amber); CRT adds a bloom (an eighth-size copy, added) and one cached mask (RGB grille, scanlines every 3 pixels, vignette); LED reads 80 rows of dots, 5 levels, a cached round-dot mask; ASCII reads 40 rows of cells (1:1.9) and draws glyphs of the ramp ` .:-=+*#%@` from an atlas, coloured by the cell's colour at full brightness; Terminal (take C) reads the frame as half-block cells (45 rows, two pixels a cell), steps the colours (8 levels per channel; hardware: a 6-step amber ramp) and draws a text interface around it (tab bar, a braille scope and an eighth-block spectrum of the song's wave, a status bar with the mode, the song, the section, the bar, the render and the tempo). On Monitor, Rullo and the layer over a scene, ASCII and Terminal are drawn natively by `src/visuals/glyph-looks.js` (the layer after the pass, `drawLayerGlyph`; with 1-bit, CRT and LED the layer is drawn before the pass and takes it) on the grid of `src/visuals/term-grid.js` (take A for Terminal: rounded panels titled in the instrument's colour, a level line, braille scopes, eighth-block spectra, half-block piano rolls, `●` punchcards, a status bar; ASCII: `+-|` frames, `[####....]` levels, slope glyphs), and no pass runs after them. The code panel never takes a render, in the page or in videos (`drawCode` is untouched). "On slow devices" (`coding-misk-render-light`: automatic, on, off) draws half the cells or dots; automatic switches after 30 frames in a row over 8 ms, live only (the fast video never switches on its own). Videos and fragments use the stage's render unless their own setting (`render` in `coding-misk-video` and `coding-misk-fragments`, "stage" by default) picks one; a video recorded from the stage draws the video's render while it records.
- **The "Claude" look** (`claude`, the same name in every language: the owner's explicit exception to the rule on brand names, rs10). Take B of the Terminal render, a look of its own: the song shown as an agent command line tool at work, drawn by `drawClaude` with the Terminal palette of the theme from the widget looks' frame: a header card (the song, the section, the tempo, the bar), what enters (the instruments playing in this section and not in the one before) and what comes next (the next section and the bars left), a large pane (the first melodic instrument with a note widget) and smaller panes for the others, a prompt box with a typed line and a block cursor (the step line typed over its first bar as the user's request, else "bring in …", "drop …" or "keep the groove" from the section change, typed over the section's first bar; these invented lines are never spoken or captioned), a status line and a mode line. Above the prompt, the reply block (spoken-text-on-stage D4, C1): the last two exchanges, each the step line on a highlighted ground, `●` and the spoken line typed over its sample's length, then `⎿ narrator · bar 8`; a voice-track line is a tool-output line; the prompt clears once the reply comes; a short stage keeps the last exchange only. The option Transcript (C2, the chip beside the Render menu on this look, `coding-misk-claude-transcript`, off by default) adds a pane on the right with the older exchanges, never in 9:16 frames. The page caption is not drawn over this look while its replies are on. It leaves room at the top for the stage's readout, as Monitor does. Terminal draws it as it is; the other renders pass over it.
- **Captions** (spoken-text-on-stage, #82, `src/captions/captions.js`). What is heard, written over the stage while it is said, from `captionSchedule` ([narrator.md](narrator.md)): one lane (`#say`) at the bottom of the stage (or the top), one row per speaker ordered by rank, each with its mark (the drawn icon, the narrator's italics in cyan, the voice track's quotes in magenta, a label; Settings → Texts on stage chooses per kind among Nothing, Formatting only, Label, Icon, Icon and formatting), the voice's name on hover, and the step line as a small tag below when it differs from what is heard (or, as the old behaviour, instead of it). `captionFrame(lines, bar, S, opts)` gives the rows of a moment; `captionHtml` draws the lane and `drawCaptions` every canvas: the session video, the fast video and fragments (`captionSchedule` of the exported song; 9:16 in the lower third, two lines, the speaker above), the timeline layout (the heard line beside the playhead) and the live output. The Studio monitor types the heard line with its speaker. HW: an amber display with a light per speaker; 400 px: 17 px, two lines. Settings in `coding-misk-captions` (in the backup): one switch, then narrator, voice track, console lines, step lines, the "Claude" look's replies, the marks, the step line's form, where, In videos too; the fragments menu and Settings → Video can turn texts off for one export.
- **Song soul.** A science fiction screen of the song playing: ten views, drawn from the song's own data, inside a choice of device shells. It opens from a hidden screw on the stage (an easter egg).
- **Procedural visuals.** Endless scrolling scenes were built and then moved out of the app at the owner's request. They live in the owner's local notes; the repo keeps the tags `visuals-v1` to `visuals-v3`.
- **Video layouts.** Where the visual, the code, the soul and the timeline sit in an exported video, and the vertical fragments.
- **Code over** (it: Codice sopra, OpenSpec change `code-over-visual`, `src/video/code-over.js`). The code drawn over the scene instead of in a panel: one band per line as wide as its text, nothing on empty lines, comments and muted lines dimmed, long lines cut with `…` (this layout only), the line being typed in the theme's accent with a block cursor (from the typing plan's `cursor(t)`, or CodeMirror's caret while typing by hand), the playing tokens framed or filled. One painter (`drawCodeOver`) for the session video (layout `code-over`), the fast video, the fragments (show `code-over`), the live output (view 14) and an optional stage mode of the page (the stage's Code over button, off by default, an overlay canvas `#stage-over`; the editor stays open). Areas (`codeOverAreas`): 16:9 from 3.5% of the width to the Code width setting (62% by default) on the left or the right, or the whole width above a bottom band; tall pictures the whole width above 84%. Captions, the song card, the stage strip and the Terminal pass's panes keep to the free side (`setLayerFree`). Without typing the scroll shows the window with the most lines lit in the last two seconds. The lit tokens are the haps' `context.locations` mapped from the code evaluated to the shown lines by their text (the fast video gets them from the render page, `lit` on the job; a line being edited lights nothing until it is evaluated).

## How it connects

- **Songs** pick a look with `look` ([songs.md](songs.md)). The radio has its own look in Settings.
- **Export** draws the same frames into video files; the fast video draws them from the timeline, so a frame depends only on the bar ([export.md](export.md)).
- **Live build** decides the code shown in video layouts with code ([live-build.md](live-build.md)).
- **Settings** keep the video layout, quality, card, visual and fragment options ([settings.md](settings.md)).

A render pass holds no state between frames and uses no random numbers: the same frame gives the same output, alone or in a sequence (`npm run check:render-styles`, `node tools/check-render-styles.cjs`). The canvas a pass reads back is made `willReadFrequently` from the first frame: Chrome moves a canvas read back a few times from the GPU to the CPU, and the first frames would be smoothed differently. Palco is not repeatable even in Normal (the clock and random sparks), so the checks leave it out of the determinism cases.

A frame must depend on the bar and the levels only, never on wall time. Then the live player, the fast video and the checks draw the same thing. The widget looks go one step further: their levels, onsets and silences come from the notes (queried live from the playing pattern once per cycle, cached; sent by the render page in the fast video), and the spectrum's columns are kept by their time slot, so a frame drawn alone is the same as one drawn in a sequence (`npm run check:stage-widgets`, `node tools/check-stage-widgets.cjs`). Live, the scope reads the instrument's own analyser; in the fast video the code widgets' renders of an instrument when there are some, else the song's wave scaled by the instrument's level (said in the export log).

## Example

Screenshots of one look playing a song, to compare before and after a change:

```sh
node tools/screenshot-visual.cjs edgerunners luci-rosse 3 /tmp/edge
```

Add a look: a drawing function in `src/visuals.js`, an entry in `LOOKS` with English and Italian names, a Guide line in the card `visuals`, and screenshots for the owner. The owner wants drawn icons, never emoji, and screenshots before any change of look ([pitfalls.md](pitfalls.md)).
