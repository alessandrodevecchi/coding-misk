# Voice: track (voice instrument)

## What it is

The voice instrument, labelled "Voice: track" (it: "Voce: traccia"; "voice track" in sentences, voice-full-controls), is a track of type `spoken`: spoken lines that are part of the music. It is not the narrator. The narrator explains the steps of a build; the voice instrument plays in the song as written, in exports and in every build.

Lines are samples made on macOS by `npm run voices`. Without them the track stays silent and the song plays on.

**Speakers and samples (real-voices).** The speakers are `albert`, `fred`, `junior` and `kathy`, English only, and `siri4` (shown as Siri 4), Italian only (`SPEAKER_LANGS` in `src/song/build.js`, copied in `tools/voice-sample.mjs`). `siri4` is a voice `say -v` cannot name: it speaks with the macOS system voice (`say` without `-v`, `SYSTEM_SPEAKERS` in `tools/voice-sample.mjs`), so set that voice as the system voice before `npm run voices`; it gets every text of `say_it` too, and its probe also compares it with Samantha (#82); the default voice is Samantha in English and Alice in Italian. A line in a language its speaker does not speak plays from the default bank (`sayBank` gives `say_<lang>`), with the track's sound. A removed speaker (`eddy`, `flo`, `grandma`, `grandpa`, `reed`, `rocko`, `sandy`, `shelley`) in saved data reads as the default voice (`speakerId`). `npm run voices` (`--jobs N`, default 4) writes no sample for a speaker whose probe line has the same audio data as the default voice's (listed by `say -v '?'` but not installed), and deletes a speaker sample that came out as a copy of the default voice's; the dev server's voice helper answers 404 `speaker not installed` then. `node tools/voice.mjs --prune` lists the banks of removed speakers and of languages a speaker does not speak; `--prune --yes` deletes exactly those. `npm run check:speakers` proves every speaker sounds different from the default voice.

## How it connects

- **My phrases (my-phrases, #82).** A line typed in the phrase list or the radio console is offered to the user's phrases (save policy in Settings → Voices and phrases). A vocabulary line of a generated song, of Fill with or New phrases may become a phrase of mine (kind `voice`, the same group or none), drawn on its own stream after the vocabulary; a song's or a style's own lines never change ([phrases.md](phrases.md)).
- **Songs.** A spoken track's patterns are lines, and its clips place them ([songs.md](songs.md)).
- **Variety (voice-track-tuning, #82).** Generated chops last at most 4 bars, every other bar, with sparse rhythms; refrains return every 4 or 8 bars, a one-refrain song at most 3 times (`times`); line A is in at most 3 mixed phrases; vocabulary lines are written out as lists of entries (each entry heard on its quarter, `n(…).s(bank)`), never repeating another line's entry; a style line one time in two. In the radio a song leaves out the lines of the 5 songs before (`sessionMemory`, [radio.md](radio.md)). `node tools/voice-repeats.mjs` measures it on a fixed seed list.
- **Code shown (voice-track-tuning D8, D11).** "V:T code" (key `spoken`) and "V:N code" (key `voice`) in Options hide the voices' code; both are off by default when the song grows, on with static code. With V:T code on in a grown song's code of the moment, each line shows from the last step in the 4 bars before its first start (a display step on the bar before when none) until the first step after its last start; the rest plays from the hidden tail, effects folded with Voice effects on. A voice line new at a step is pasted at the end of the typing, or typed in a free gap ([live-build.md](live-build.md)).
- **Narrator.** The narrator yields: it stays quiet on bars where a line sounds and never announces a spoken track ([narrator.md](narrator.md)).
- **On the stage (spoken-text-on-stage, #82).** Every start of a voice-track line shows as a caption in quotes, magenta, while it is heard, at least 3 s unless the next voice-track line replaces it (`captionHold`, the narrator's rule; a chop once per clip, held for the clip, a refrain at each repeat), also while the user codes by hand; the lines typed in the radio console are voice-track lines marked `typed` (their own choice in Settings → Texts on stage) ([visuals.md](visuals.md)). The caption is what the code plays (`lineEntries`, `src/song/spoken.js`): a vocabulary line of several entries is written `n("a b c ~").s(bank)` and plays every entry on its quarter of the bar, so its captions and its Coming next row show each entry as it is heard, never the entries joined with " · " (the code's comment still quotes them all).
- **Typed lines on grown songs (fx3).** On a radio song grown on air (Live coder, Crescendo) a line typed in the console goes on the grown song at its own bar, after the other commands (`steerOnAir`, `src/endless/waypoints.js`); it does not go through the director's written form, whose clips the transit keeps only from the arrival bar. Say now takes the first bar that starts at least `SAY_LEAD` seconds after the click (`airBoundaries` and `applyBar` with a `lead` in bars, half a bar at least; the steering keeps that bar for a typed line), and the radio puts the line on the song on air at once (`sayAir` in `command`, `src/radio/radio.js`) instead of waiting for the steering away from the page, which can answer after the line's bar (measured with the CPU slowed 4 times: the line was never started).
- **A typed line never blocks the page (#82).** Say now and At the right moment did their work in one block of the main thread (0.15 to 0.34 s with the CPU slowed 4 times): the console, my phrases and the song in Compose drawn and grown again on the click, then the new radio window wrote the Live coder's written plan (`planWritten`) in one go, twice (again on the steering's answer), searching each bank's file list with `decodeURI` for every line. Now a bank's files are indexed by slug once (`slugAt`, `src/song/build.js`); the plan is made a little at a time before the window goes on air (`planWrittenSteps`, `prepare` of `liveCode` and `airCode`, `swapSoon` in `src/radio/radio.js`), at the latest a third of a bar before the line's bar; the steering's answer that gives the song already on air swaps nothing; my phrases, the Compose growth (away from the page, `composeCompiled`), the radio's history and the soundfonts of the window go in tasks of their own. A second pass (fix/82-say-lag-2) took the rest off the click: the line is tried first and queued only once its window is ready (`placeLine` in `src/radio/radio.js`: after its sample, the song with the line, `windowPlayable({ item, song })`, its plan made a little at a time, then `command(c, pre)` swaps that window), so nothing is redone and the window never goes on air before its plan; a window waiting for its plan gives way to a newer one (`swapSoon` keeps the earlier bar and stops the older slices), also for the next song's window (`prepareNext`) and Cancel; only the new sample's bank is registered again (`reloadSamples(bank)`); the soundfonts of a Classic window are found step by step; my phrases redraw the Voices tab, the radio console (not the whole tab) and the song in Compose (when idle) in tasks of their own, also on the Undo of "Saved in your phrases". `tools/check-say-lag.cjs` keeps every task under 100 ms at that rate, the Undo included.
- **Say now in a few seconds (#82, owner, 2 November).** Say now means "in a few seconds": the line goes on the first bar that starts at least `SAY_LEAD` (2) seconds after the click, shown in the queue from the click ("sample on its way"). When the sample or the window with the line is not ready a third of a bar before that bar, the line moves to the first bar `SAY_LEAD` seconds ahead again (a push; also when the sample came with less than half the lead left); after `SAY_PUSHES` (3) pushes its window is finished at once, so the line is never lost. At the right moment works the same way from that bar. The queue and Coming next show the bar the line really goes on (`heardAt`, `lineBarOf`).
- **The radio's window (fx1).** The window part of a song grown on air carries `spokenGrowth`, the bar its growth ends in the window's bars (`windowPart`, `src/live/air-code.js`), so `placeSpoken` places a refrain of the growth as on the song alone. Without it the code that plays had no start for those refrains while the captions and Coming next, read from the song alone, showed them. `npm run check:voice-heard` (Node) and `tools/check-voice-heard.cjs` (browser) compare what is shown with what plays.
- **Growth shapes.** The `voice` opening starts a Live coder build with the first opening line ([growth-shapes.md](growth-shapes.md)).
- **Styles.** A recipe's `spoken` field gives the chance, the ways, lines, speakers and a vocabulary group ([styles.md](styles.md)).
- **Live build.** A spoken track is never thinned. It enters on the bar of its first line ([live-build.md](live-build.md)).
- **Grown on air (#81, after-tests-oct28 D3).** A radio song in Crescendo or Live coder grows from a bar of its written form (`growOnAir`). Its spoken tracks are then placed again over the whole grown song (`placeGrownSpoken` in `src/endless/voice-track.js`): the director's patterns and number of clips, entry lines (`before`) before their bound track first sounds, the others on parts of the kinds the track may speak in (`grownParts`), quiet first and spread (`pickParts`). The growth counts too: a phrase of the growth is quiet when at most half the most tracks play there and at least one does, by the build's state at its bars (its clips run under its steps, so its sections look busy by clips). With too few parts of the kinds allowed, the quietest others take the rest, so the number of lines stays. A refrain placed in the growth repeats there (`placeSpoken` reads `crescendo.growth` of a song with `onAir`). The add step moves to the first clip. Compose is unchanged. Measured: pure-trance Live coder songs speak their first line at 4% to 23% of the song (were 65% to 71%).
- **Generated songs (voice-controls).** The director gives a song at most one spoken track (`words`). The voice instrument options (`voice` in the director options, `src/endless/voice-options.js`) set how often, the ways and how many phrases; the radio and the new song from an artist show them ([radio.md](radio.md)).
- **The song being edited (voice-placement).** The Compose Voices group and the panel of a spoken track show the same controls for that track (no How often: the track exists), with New phrases. A change rewrites the track with `rewriteSpoken` (`src/endless/voice-track.js`): lines drawn like the director's from the song's styles and written out, one clip per quiet part (an 8-bar phrase of a quiet section), never two in one part, the count a ceiling, the settings and speaker kept; the options stay on the track as `voiceOptions` (`draw` counts New phrases). A track without them shows options read from its clips. The toast offers Undo. A song with no spoken track offers Add a voice instrument.
- **The song on air (voice-placement).** In the radio a voice instrument change also queues a `voice` steering command on the song on air: from the first quiet part after the next phrase, the clips before it kept; the plan keeps it (`voiceAir`) so later commands, Replay and saved sessions rebuild the same song ([radio-commands.md](radio-commands.md)).

- **Sound (voice-full-controls).** The Voices group shows a folded Sound part per voice: the voice style, the speaker and eight controls (volume, pitch, tempo, filter, low cut, drive, reverb, delay), with the ranges of the track panel. Every place writes one value, the voice track's `settings` (`setVoiceSetting` in `src/main.js`), so the group and the panel always agree. Voice styles (`VOICE_STYLES` in `src/song/format.js`): `clear`, `radio`, `distant`, `robot`, `deep`, `bright`, `cathedral`, `echo`, `dirty`, `slow`; each sets the seven sound keys and never the speaker or the volume; `styleOf` gives the style the values match, else `custom`. Each drawn character on the default voice equals its style (check:spoken). In the hardware theme the Sound controls are faders (`data-fader`, `src/hardware.js`).
- **Allowed pairs (voice-style-combos, #82).** `src/voices/combos.json` lists, per language (`en`, `it`) and per speaker (`default` is the default voice: Samantha, Alice), the voice styles an automatic draw may use; a character is allowed when the style of the same name is. No entry allows every style, `clear` is always allowed, and the file ships with every style for every speaker (a template the owner narrows by ear). A draw is language independent: a speaker must allow the style in every language where it can be heard, so an English-only speaker is also bound by `it.default` (`allowedForDraw` in `src/voices/combos.js`). The narrator draw (`voiceFor`, artist weights included), the voice instrument draw and, for a chosen narrator style with no fixed speaker, the speaker draw follow it; filtering never changes how many random values a draw takes, so the music stays. Nothing allowed gives no character (`clear`). Hand choices are never restricted: the style pickers of the track panel and the Sound parts mark a style not allowed for the current speaker with ` ·` and a tip, and the Voices tab bench says whether its pair is allowed. Recipe ranges and the wild knob values are not bounded. `npm run check:voice-combos` validates the file.
- **Overrides from the app (voice-combos-ui, #82).** Groove lab, Voices, Voices and sounds: each voice card has a folded **Draw styles** grid, one toggle per style (`clear` locked on, a dot on a toggle that differs from the file, "n of 10 allowed" in the summary, **Reset to defaults** while the voice has overrides). A speaker's card edits its own language's entry (`en.<speaker>`); a style on there but off in `it.default` is marked "limited by Alice" (the intersection above). The default voice's card edits `en.default` and `it.default` together. The browser keeps only the changed speakers under `coding-misk-voice-combos` (format 1, the shape of `combos.json`, a full list per entry; an entry equal to the file is dropped). The **effective table** is the file with those entries replaced (`effectiveCombos`, `normalizeOverrides` in `src/voices/combos.js`; `src/voices/combos-store.js` sets the module table at start and on each change, event `combos-changed`); the draws, the picker marks and the bench read it. A bad stored value never throws: unknown ids, speakers and languages are dropped. **Sessions:** a radio session started while the effective table differs from the file records it in its recipe (`recipe.combos`, like `recipe.mine`); every radio draw gets `opts.combos` (`withCombos` in `src/endless/items.js`): the recorded table, else the file, never the current overrides, so a change on air applies from the next session and Replay, a saved session and its export draw as when it started. The backup carries the key; an import merges per language and speaker, the backup's entry winning. To make a set of overrides the new default, copy the entries from a backup into `src/voices/combos.json` by hand and run `npm run check:voice-combos`.
- **Phrase list (voice-full-controls D5, D6, D16).** In the Phrases part of the Voice: track block and in the track panel: one row per clip, sorted by bar (`phraseRows`, `src/endless/phrase-list.js`), with the bars where it is said, the way, the text and its sample. Text, bars and way change in place; every edit is one song edit with Undo. In Crescendo and Live coder the rows show the bar heard (written + the growth `G`); a bar typed there is stored as written, a bar inside the growth is refused. "Fill with" gives each row an unused entry of a vocabulary (dark, calm, machine, night), "+ Add line" adds a clip in the next free part Where allows, the bin removes one (the track stays, also empty).
- **Where (D7).** `voiceOptions.where` (Compose) and `voice.where` (radio): quiet, medium and busy parts, any combination, quiet only by default. Sections are classified in `sectionFacts` (`kinds`): quiet as before, busy with three quarters of the most tracks or named drop or peak, medium the rest. Lines go to the kinds allowed, quiet first, each kind spread (`wherePartsOf`, `pickParts`); refrains repeat in those kinds. The default places lines exactly as before.
- **Samples of new lines (D8).** On the dev server a helper (`vite.config.js`, `/voice/status`, `/voice/say`) makes the sample of a line written or edited in the page with `makeSample` (`tools/voice-sample.mjs`, `say` + ffmpeg, about half a second a line); the page reads the samples again without a reload. Elsewhere the row says how to make it (`npm run voices`, which also takes `--song <file>`).
- **Every style (D14).** Every recipe has a `spoken` block: 0.3 for ambient, dark-cyberpunk and pure-trance, 0.15, 0.1 or 0.05 for the others, 0 for country. The draw stays on its own stream: the music never moves. In the radio How often is As the style says, Often or Always (Sempre: every next song, whatever the weight, the complexity or the artist); the Voice: track switch is the one way to silence it (a saved Never reads as the switch off).

**Texts.** The song's own text, else the style's lines for that way, else a line from the vocabulary (`src/song/vocabulary.js`), drawn with the song's seed. At most 12 words.

**Speakers.** The track's `speaker`, else one of the style's speakers, else any speaker. Never the narrator's.

## Options

- `opening`: the line once, from the clip's first bar. With `before`, it ends one beat before a track comes in.
- `passage`: a longer text, once, at the start of the quietest section of the clip, lightly treated.
- `refrain`: the line every `every` bars, only in quiet sections, only when it ends before the section does.
- `chop`: the line cut in `slices` and played on the steps of `rhythm`, every bar of the clip.

A quiet section has at most half the tracks of the fullest one, or a name like intro, break or outro. Spoken lines play on orbits 13 and 14.

### Options for generated songs

`voice: { phrases, kind, ways, presence }` in the director options. `voiceOption` gives `null` for the default, and the session recipe carries the options only when they are not the default, so a replay sounds the same.

| Option | Values | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `phrases` | `1`, `2-3`, `4-5`, `6-8`, `many` (9 to 12) | How many phrases at most, drawn in the range |
| `kind` | `different`, `repeated`, `mixed` | A new line each time; line A every time (a refrain); A, a new line, A, another one… |
| `ways` | `null` (the style's ways and weights) or a list of `opening`, `refrain`, `chop`, `passage` | The ways a phrase may use, each as likely |
| `presence` | `never`, `style` (the style's weight), `often` (twice the weight, at most 0.9), `always` | How often a song gets one. `always` is a single song with the voice instrument on: a style without one gets every way, a vocabulary line and a speaker of the styles that have one (their vocabulary samples exist) |

Presets (`VOICE_PRESETS`): As the style (`1`, `different`, the style's ways, `style`: the default, the director's draws before voice-controls), Discreet (`1`, `different`, all four ways, `style`), Present (`2-3`, `mixed`, `often`), Obsessive (`6-8`, `repeated`, refrain and chop, `often`). A single song keeps its own on/off (`style`, `always`, `never`); the radio offers `style`, `often` and `always`, and a preset never replaces `always`. Changing a control makes it Custom (`presetOf`).

Several phrases: one pattern per line (A, B, C…) and one clip per phrase, each in a quiet part: a phrase of the plan (8 bars or so) with the role intro, break or outro, or with few tracks playing (at most half the song's usual most, at least 2). Never two phrases in one part; the parts are spread over the song; a refrain only goes to an intro, a break or an outro. A phrase that finds no part is dropped: the count is a ceiling. New lines are the style's lines for their way not used yet, else vocabulary entries written out, so two lines are never the same. The draws for the extra phrases come from their own stream (`spoken-more/<index>`): the default draws exactly as before.

## Example

A spoken track with a chopped word:

```json
{"id": "words", "type": "spoken", "settings": {"speaker": "fred", "gain": 0.55}, "patterns": {"chop": {"way": "chop", "text": {"en": "signal", "it": "segnale"}, "slices": 8, "rhythm": "0 ~ 1 ~ 2 3 ~ 3"}}, "clips": [{"start": 16, "bars": 8, "pattern": "chop"}]}
```

It plays the word in eight slices on that rhythm, every bar from bar 17 to 24. The Voices example (`songs/examples/06-voices.json`) uses all four ways.
