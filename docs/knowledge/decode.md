# Decode

## What it is

The Decode tab plays a track of the user's and writes its code while it plays (Live, level A) or from a whole-file analysis (Full, level B). The file stays on the device: the app keeps its analysis, settings and, where the browser gives one, a handle to the file.

The library around it (decode-library) is the "Another file" panel: "Choose a file…", the recent tracks and the files of the user's folders in one compact list, Autoplay, Previous and Next on the player bar, and mixtapes. The same list shows under the drop area of the empty tab.

## How it connects

- **The decode store** (`src/decode/store.js`, IndexedDB `coding-misk-decodes`, version 2): one record per file fingerprint, and a light index entry next to it (`index` store: name, title, mark, analysis version, where the file came from, last use). The lists, the marks, the order by recent, the cap and the backup's default read only the index. A record is read when its track opens, when Autoplay prepares it, or for the backup. Record and entry are written and deleted in one transaction; the index is rebuilt in the background when the two stores differ.
- **The library database** (`src/decode/library-store.js`, `coding-misk-decode-library`): folders with their handles, the folders' file index (path, size, date, tags read), single files of mixtapes, mixtapes. No audio, no decode data.
- **Folders** (`src/decode/folders.js`): the File System Access API (Chrome, Edge). The browser's permission is asked only inside a click; after a reload a folder's last rows show at once, waiting. The scan is depth first, sorted by name, skips dot names, keeps the extensions Decode opens, stops at 2000 files per folder and pauses every 100 entries.
- **Kinds and videos without sound:** every row has a kind icon from `src/icons.js` (a music note, a video, a crossed speaker for a video without sound). A video without an audio track (a screen recording) is not a broken file: the idle tag read (or opening it) stores `video: true, audio: false` in the folder index and the row keeps the normal style. It plays in silence for the length read from its header, on a silent clock (`createSilentClock`), with a comment placeholder in the code column (nothing evaluated, no record stored). With the Video look the stage shows the picture of the video file Decode plays, in time with its clock ([visuals.md](visuals.md)).
- **One rule for rows that cannot play** (`openWithSkips` in `tab.js`): Previous, Next and a mixtape's Play skip rows "not found" or waiting without opening them, skip a row that fails when opened, name the skipped rows in one note, and stop with a note when nothing playable is left (no loop with repeat).
- **Marks** ◆ Full and ◇ Live come from the index: a record remembers `where` (folder, path, size, date) so a folder's file is marked without reading it. A stale analysis (older `ANALYSIS_VERSION`) has no mark.
- **Autoplay** (`src/decode/autoplay.js`): the next row of the list or mixtape the track came from is prepared 30 s before the end (read, decoded within a 120 MB budget, fingerprint, stored record, a second level A worker). The hand-over starts the next original where the playing one ends; not ready, the tab waits, and after 3 s the sound starts and the code joins when the tempo is found.
- **Settings backup** ([library.md](library.md), [settings.md](settings.md)): the 100 analyses used last by default, every analysis with "Include all Decode analyses" (written in parts); mixtapes with folder names and paths, never handles.
- **Sounds by measure in level B** (decode-refine D3, "Our sounds"): the drum machine is the one whose closed hat has the nearest spectral shape (twelfth-octave bands from 2 to 16 kHz, the tilt taken out; machines within 0.02 are told apart by the hats' brightness), measured on the hats that play without a kick or a clap, against `src/decode/kits.js` (made by `node --no-warnings tools/decode-kits.mjs` from the machines' samples). A bass whose brightness moves over the bar is a sawtooth with a moving resonant filter. Chords still sounding at the end of their bar get `.room(0.4)`; the Lead's wave and filter follow the brightness. The part levels: each part's gain (drums included) is scaled so the code's band shares come nearest the original's, in five bands (20-150, 150-400, 400-1000, 1000-2000, 2000-16000 Hz) and in the similarity check's three, predicted from `src/decode/part-levels.js` (each sound alone, measured through the app by `tools/decode-levels.cjs`: the drums per machine and row and hit, the bass and the Lead per sounding step) times the hits and steps the code plays; among the gains within 0.05 the smallest change wins; when no gains bring every one of the three bands within 0.10, the nearest gains are still taken if they bring the largest share difference down by 0.03 or more (round 8), else the levels stay as written. Where a Lead plays over at least half of the chords' bars, the chords lose their body under 1200 Hz (`.hpf(1200).lpf(4000)`, `cutUnderLead`, after the balance), so the pad does not cover the Lead's and the drums' onsets in the mid band. A driven bass is detected by its even harmonics against its odd ones (2nd, 4th, 6th over 3rd, 5th, 7th, at the decoded pitch, on the bass steps without a kick or a clap; `bassDrive` in `kit-measure.js`, `DRIVE_RULE`): a clip turns a sawtooth toward a square, which holds odd harmonics only, so a driven bass reads -4.8 to -5.9 dB and a clean one -1.3 to +5.3; with the fundamental's share of the first 12 harmonics it picks the nearest of the app's own basses (`bassDriveOf`), and a drive of 3 or more is written as `.distort(d).distortvol(0.5)` after the filter, a moving filter's resonance at 4 instead of 8 (`SOUND_RULE.drive`), and the balance moves it by its `distortvol` (the clip makes the level almost independent of the gain before it). The drums' drive (round 8): the level table holds every machine's rows driven at 1, 2 and 3; after the balance, when the original's 2-16 kHz share is at least 1.3 times the clean kit's prediction, the drums get the drive whose rows bring the prediction nearest, only if nearer than the clean kit by 0.3 in natural log (`DRUM_DRIVE.margin`), so a file near the edge keeps its decision. The hats' brightness against the machine's sample does not tell a driven kit from a clean one on the bench (driven 0.95 to 1.05, two clean kits the rule drives 0.89 and 0.97), so it gates nothing. The bass profiles (`src/decode/bass-profiles.js`, `tools/decode-bass-profiles.cjs`) are measured over drums without the pad, at cutoffs 300, 700 and 1200: a square is written when the bass reads -15 dB even/odd or under, a sine from a fundamental share of 0.91, and a sawtooth's drive from the rows at cutoff 700 (a clean sawtooth in a mix reads a lower share than alone, so the clean rows do not choose a cutoff or a supersaw). `node --no-warnings tools/decode-kits.mjs --holdout` checks the machine on held-out songs never used to choose the rule (`tools/decode-export.cjs --holdout` exports them); `tools/decode-similarity.cjs --holdout` prints their similarity. "Simple kit" is unchanged.
- **The bench** (decode-refine D1): the test songs of `tools/decode-songs.mjs` (with the acid track and the arp-and-lead track, whose truth holds the lead notes) and the app's songs `luci-rosse` and one radio song frozen in `tests/snapshots/decode-radio-song.json`, exported by `tools/decode-export.cjs` with their timeline JSON. `tools/decode-measure.mjs` reads the hit times and sections of the timelines as truth; `tools/decode-similarity.cjs` fast-exports each file's level B song and compares its sound with the original against `tests/snapshots/decode-similarity.json` (a drop over 0.05 fails; the arc is the correlation of the loudness per bar with a 1 dB floor on each curve's spread, so a flat song scores near 0, and two runs give the same scores because the fast export is repeatable, decode-refine 11.3). The similarity targets of the spec are not met yet: the snapshot holds today's scores so the later groups can only improve them.
- **Playlists** hold generated songs; mixtapes hold references to the user's files. A decoded track reaches a playlist only through Open in Compose and a save.

## Analysis and writer rules (decode-refine part 2)

Groups 6 to 9 of `decode-refine`. Each rule keeps its numbers in one constant, so it can be tuned after listening; `ANALYSIS_VERSION` (`src/decode/model.js`) is 5, and a stored analysis of an older version has no mark and runs again.

- **Kick under the bass** (`KICK_RULE`, misk-analyzer): a flux peak in the kick band (at least 0.45 of the threshold) with a low-band rise on all four beats of a bar is a kick even where the pitch-fall test reads a bass note. The broadband click of the design does not tell a distorted acid bass from a kick: both attack in the 1-5 kHz band.
- **Hats and claps** (`HAT_RULE`, `CLAP_RULE`): a hat step is on above half the median of the strong hats of the last 4 bars. A clap must be darker than 1.3 times the lower third of the hat ratios of the last 6 seconds, reach half the strong hits of the snare band, and be noisy (spectral flatness at least 0.3; a synth note on the beat measures 0.11 to 0.22).
- **Level B main grid** (`GRID_RULE`, writer): a step is on when it is on in at least 60 % of the section's bars with drums; a variant must come back in at least 10 % of them.
- **Level A relock** (`RELOCK_BARS` 8): after 8 bars with kicks, level A measures tempo and phase again on those bars. A tempo in a ratio to the old one (half, two thirds, three halves, double) replaces it only with the claps' off-beat; the same tempo moves the phase by half a beat (kicks on the off eighths) or a sixteenth (hats only on odd sixteenths); a whole tempo within 0.1 BPM replaces the one found. Never after a tapped, picked or stored tempo. The tab starts the code again on the new grid (`relock` in the trail).
- **Chord quality per section** (`steadyQualities`, `HARMONY_RULE`): the section's majority for each root, unless the other quality has a confidence above 0.8 in at least two bars; a tie goes to the key's quality. A bar whose chroma shows a weak minor third and no major third (marked `third` by misk-analyzer) is minor where the key holds that minor chord (`quietMinors`).
- **Bass range** (`bassRange`): one window of 15 semitones per section, the one that holds the most notes; notes outside move by octaves; no notes in bars where the bass is below 20 % of the section's loudest. A wide supersaw bass is read with a looser YIN threshold (`BASS_RULE` 0.35) only on the step after a kick.
- **Phantom parts** (`PARTS_RULE`, `full.js`, round 8): a file whose Essentia.js tempo confidence is under 0.4 has no steady beat (`model.beat.steady` false): level B clears its drum hits and writes no drums (a pad-only bench song reads -0.11, every bench file with drums 1.6 or more, a sample of real tracks 0.87 or more). Decoded bass notes are no bass when the original's 20-150 Hz share is under 0.3 and the notes have almost no energy at their own fundamental (the bass profile's `h1` under 0.1): level B clears them (`model.noBass`) and writes no bass. Without Essentia.js nothing changes. The tempo is not touched.
- **Bass notes under a kick and held notes** (`BASS_RULE`, misk-analyzer, round 8): a step with a kick that reads as a kick alone is read again from 45 % of the step on, at YIN's looser threshold; it is a bass note when its late level rises by 15 % over the step before's, and the line confirms the pitch (the same pitch within two steps, or in a second late window). A step's two pitch windows agree within a semitone (a square bass under a low-pass reads about a semitone apart). A step where the note before still sounds (the same pitch, no new attack, above 12 % of the loudest bass step) is a hold (`bars[].bassHold`) while its level stays at 60 % of the note's attack through the step (a short note's ringing tail is no hold); level B writes it as `_`, and the level prediction counts a held step as 0.8 of a sounding step (measured through the app on held sine, sawtooth and square notes: 0.75 held four steps, 0.86 held a bar), so a held sub bass is one long note, not a blip. A step that would hold the note but whose level rises to 1.15 times (1.2 before round 9) the end of the step before is the same note struck again (`BASS_RULE.again`): a rolling or acid line repeats its pitch with an attack, a held note only decays. Bench: bass note recall 0.52 to 0.64 and precision 0.67 to 0.70 over 34 songs, the true bass's sounding steps covered 0.34 to 0.45.
- **The tempo vote** (`TEMPO_VOTE`, `full.js`, round 8): Essentia.js also gives Percival's and Degara's tempo; when the three agree within 4 % and the tempo picked is another, theirs wins. A pick at double their tempo moves only above confidence 3.5 (a double-time groove and a half-time one look alike), a pick at half of it from confidence 2.0 when the three agree as read; for a move that is no octave (3:2), an estimate an octave off still counts as agreeing.
- **Power chords** (`POWER_RULE`, writer, round 8): a section whose chords fail the key test on their qualities but whose roots lie in the key's scale or a semitone over its tonic (the flat second of a phrygian riff) on at least 80 % of its bars, on the tonic or that flat second on half of them and on the tonic itself on at least 15 %, with a second root on at least 15 %, is written as power chords (`chord("E5")`, a root and a fifth): a distorted power chord reads major, its fifth harmonic a major third. A section of chords with roots out of the key stays out (noise of the drums in the chord band).
- **Round 9 rules:** the beat phase checked on the claps (`PHASE_RULE`, `clapShift` in `full.js`): when at least 60 % of a file's claps (16 or more) sit on one other sixteenth of the beat, the grid moves there (a rolling bass on the offbeats read as kicks put the beat an eighth off). A kick-band hit with no pitch read twice whose 150-700 Hz energy is at least 0.4 of its 40-130 Hz energy is a bass note's attack, not a kick, unless it rises as the track's strong kicks do (`KICK_RULE.harm`, `strong`). The tempo vote takes the middle of the three estimates when RhythmExtractor2013's own is more than 1.5 % from it (`TEMPO_VOTE.middle`). A tonic with neither third in the chroma without the bass's harmonics takes its mode from the scale degrees of the bass and the chord roots (`MODE_RULE`, `thirdlessMode`). A quiet bass bar keeps notes on its chord's root (`HARMONY_RULE.rootNotes`). A machine whose hat falls off above 11 kHz by more than the track's hats (`KIT_RULE.top`, `hatTop`) is not picked: the code had no 11-16 kHz octave on seven real tracks. Power chords only where drums or bass play on half the section's bars (`POWER_RULE.riff`). The chords' wave and a sung melody's Lead are chosen by how near the balance can bring the band shares (`soundsByFit`, `SOUND_RULE.pick`): the chords may leave the triangle for a sawtooth or a supersaw, and a sung melody may play instead of starting muted.
- **Round 11 rules** (decode-hit-rhythm): level B measures per bar with a chord the chord's own tones step by step (`chordHits` in `src/decode/hit-measure.js`: bins of the chord's pitch classes between 300 and 2500 Hz counted by how far they stand over their neighbours, the tones the bar's bass rings at left out; the hits, the held measure and the tonal share go into the model as `chordHits`, `chordHeld`, `chordTonal`). A section whose chords stand out of the band (tonal share 0.1 or more), are held under 0.4, hit at least 3 times a bar where no bass note starts, and come back on the same steps (overlap 0.65 or more) is written with `.struct("…")` after the voicing, per block of 4 bars (`CHORD_RULE`); a detuned supersaw pad under loud drums reads nothing reliable and stays held. A bass that attacks in the 300-1500 Hz band on its note steps is written again on the empty or held steps where it attacks, and on a kick's step between two notes whose second half holds as much as theirs (`bassAttack`, `BASS_FILL`, `bassFilled`). Two sections of the same kind with the same levels per part and brightness share their parts: the drums' pattern with a kick, and a bass or chords part one of them lacks (`TWIN_RULE`, `twinParts`). The bench measures hits per bar and how held each band is, original against code, and each part's onsets against the true code (`tools/decode-hits.mjs`), with six songs built for this (`HIT_SONGS`).
- **Round 12 rules** (decode-round12, writer only): a bar's chord is heard at any level when its tones stand out of the band (`chordTonal` 0.2 or more) and no line plays sixteenths there, and a section heard mostly so is written at a gain scaled by the square root of its chord level (`QUIET_RULE`, `chordsHeardB`): quiet intros, breaks and outros are no longer silent. A section that fails the key test with such tones is written as power chords. A section whose chords are, on most bars, the major chord on the main note of a driven or bright bass playing sixteenths (`LINE_RULE`, `lineChord`) writes no chords, unless a quarter of its bars read that note as a minor chord or the line moves over more than two roots. In a section whose bars read a clap on every beat a 16th after the kicks, those claps are the kick's click and become the kick (`KICK_CLICK`, `kickClicks`); hats on 3 and 11 or 5 and 13 alone are a backbeat snare a 16th off the grid and become claps (`OFF_SNARE`, `offSnare`). The balance reads a part's level from its loudest pattern. The bench reports the bars where the code is silent and the original plays (`silentBars` in `tools/decode-hits.mjs`).
- **Round 13 rules** (decode-round13): the song goes on past the model's ending over a pad that fades out, bars whose chord reads with confidence, a tonal share of 0.2 and a chord level not nil (`TAIL_RULE`, `tailEnd`); a quiet section with no chord heard and few drums that holds one root of the key on most bars, at a mean tonal share of 0.1, writes a quiet power chord there (`DRONE_RULE`, `droneOf`); a section's first bars (8 or more) whose chord tones never hold while later bars hold twice as much and louder write no chords: the drums' noise read as chords before a pad enters (`LATE_RULE`, `lateChords`). Level B measures the kick's attack per step (`kickShape` in `hit-measure.js`: the 90-250 Hz and 4-12 kHz rise at each 16th step); in a loud section (drums level 0.6 or more) whose band rises on one step of every beat by 6 dB and 3 dB over the other steps, when another section of the song has four kicks a bar on that step, the section gets four kicks a bar there and loses the claps a 16th before them (`FULL_KICK`, `fullKick`, `fourOnFloor`). Tried and dropped: telling one line from a chord by the tones sounding together or by one note's harmonics ([decisions.md](decisions.md)).
- **The chords' room** (`SOUND_RULE.roomRange`, writer, round 8): the chords get a room from 0.3 (chords that stop within their bar) to 0.9 (chords that ring to its end), along the measured sustain; before, 0.4 from a sustain of 0.6 and none under it.
- **The whole tempo** (`WHOLE_RULE`, `full.js`, round 10): a line fit within 0.2 BPM of a whole tempo that RhythmExtractor2013 puts within 0.15 BPM of it is fitted again with the whole tempo allowed (misk-analyzer `fitGrid(bpm, phase, wholeNear)`), kept when the fit is as good: a 6-minute real track fitted 111.17 where it plays at 111 and drifted by half a second. A wider reach (0.25) also moved a track fitted 109.25 to 109, with its band error up 1.5 dB.
- **The learned beat tracker, an option off by default** (`BEAT_RULE`, `trackerGrid` in `full.js`, `beat-this.js`, `engine-beat-this.js`, round 10, decode-beat-tracker): `analyseFull(pcm, { beatTracker })` takes the grid from Beat This! (MIT, ONNX, onnxruntime-web, one thread) when its beats are steady and relate to the picked tempo (same, half, double, 3:2); the picked octave stays, misk-analyzer's onset phase stays unless the tracker's lies half a beat away, the downbeat from its downbeats. The round 10 verdict (bench better, real tracks and the owner's files not, three real tracks worse by more than 0.5 dB) keeps it from being the default. Round 12 ships it as an option of Settings, off by default (Settings → Deep beat analysis, [settings.md](settings.md)): turned on and downloaded, the tab sends `beats` to the level B worker, which reads the files from Cache Storage, checks their SHA-256 and only then imports onnxruntime-web (`engine-beat-this-browser.js`, the `.wasm` served by the app, one thread, no proxy worker); a stored level B analysis made without it is made again; the panel adds "grid: Beat This!" and the progress a "beats" stage. Off, nothing of it is requested. Local override for the owner's dev server: a git-excluded `vite.local.config.mjs` with `define: { 'import.meta.env.VITE_DECODE_BEAT_TRACKER': JSON.stringify('on') }` starts the option on (the files still need the download). The spec and its design are in `openspec/changes/decode-beat-tracker/`. Browser check: `tools/check-beat-tracker.cjs` (`--flag` with that config). Bench: `decode-bench.cjs --beat-this [small]`, the model files in `.cache/beat-this/`.
- **Top line and Lead** (`full.js`, `LEAD_RULE`): Essentia.js's `PredominantPitchMelodia` (150-2100 Hz), shifted by `MELODY_LAG` (60 ms), or misk-analyzer's YIN on the 200-2000 Hz band without Essentia.js; one note per sixteenth snapped to the key's scale within 40 cents. Its voicing drives the `vox`/`melody` lines only in bars where part 1's `top.up` hears a top line. The writer adds a `lead` code track, one loop of 1, 2 or 4 bars per section in scale degrees (`leadCode`), with no Lead in a section voiced on less than 30 % of its steps; under `vox` lines it starts muted (`hush`; `_$:` in Compose), unless the sounds by fit hear it (round 9). Level A writes no Lead. Known limit: under a louder pad or arpeggio MELODIA follows them (13 % right on the arp-and-lead test song, 89 % on a hook over drums).

**Similarity check** (`tools/decode-similarity.cjs`, the measures in `tools/decode-similarity.mjs`): each bench file is decoded in level B, its song fast-exported by the app and compared with the original on the original's bar grid: rhythm per band (low 30-150 Hz, mid 150-2000 Hz, high 5-16 kHz), chroma, bass chroma, brightness ratio, band shares, the loudness arc, and two floor pairs (unrelated files) for scale. A score that drops more than 0.05 under `tests/snapshots/decode-similarity.json` fails; `--write` updates the snapshot. The spec's targets on the app songs and the test songs: low-band and high-band rhythm at least 0.5, chroma at least 0.85, bass chroma at least 0.8, brightness ratio between 0.75 and 1.33, each band's share within 0.10 of the original's. Not met yet: brightness and band shares wait for the sounds by measure (group 10). The arc of flat test songs moves between exports (task 11.3).

**Edit measures** (`tools/decode-edits.mjs`, D7): targets 10-30 % of bars edited, 2-8 edits per minute at 120-130 BPM, an average edit of at least 20 characters, at most 15 % small edits, at least 90 % of level B's section changes edited within one bar. The numbers per file are in the design (D7) and the DEVLOG.

## Options

| Option | Values | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Autoplay | off (default), on; remembered in `coding-misk-decode-autoplay` | the panel, browsers with folders |
| Folder chip | All or one folder; remembered with the order in `coding-misk-decode-list` | the panel |
| Order | Name, Recent | the panel |
| Kind | All, Audio, Video (a video without sound is a video), with counts from the rows in memory; remembered with the chip and the order (`media` in `coding-misk-decode-list`), part of the context of Autoplay, Previous and Next | the panel |
| Search | name, artist or file name, case and accents ignored; not remembered | the panel |
| Mixtape repeat | off (default), on; kept with the mixtape | an open mixtape |
| Include all Decode analyses | off by default and on every visit of the settings page | Settings, backup card |

Numbers: 2000 files listed per folder, 2000 stored analyses (Live-only ones dropped first), about 20 kB of analysis per minute of audio, 100 analyses in a default backup, 30 s of preparation, a 120 MB budget for the prepared track's sound, 3 s of silence before a late next track's sound starts.

## Example

A folder "Demos" ordered by name, Autoplay on: the user opens "B" from the Demos chip. About 30 s before B ends, the next Demos track by name is read and decoded and its Live analysis starts. At B's end its sound starts exactly where B's ends (the hand-over skips the few milliseconds of its start the code's clock has passed) and the code column starts from the new track's first lines. Next on the player bar does the same at once; Previous opens the track before.
